01

About

I am a postdoctoral associate in Mechanical Engineering at MIT, working with Prof. Ming Guo and Prof. Roger D. Kamm. I received my Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering from MIT in 2024, advised by Prof. Guo.

My work integrates mechanics, biophysics, and bioengineering to understand living tissues as systems of cells and fluid. I combine custom micromechanics instruments, imaging, and computational modeling to measure how tissues deform, relax, and transport fluid.

My long-term goal is to connect these physical measurements to biological function and develop quantitative approaches for studying aging, cancer, and neurodegenerative disease.

02

Research

Understanding living materials through
mechanics, fluid flow, and engineering.

Tissue mechanics & transport

Fluid flow as a mechanism
of tissue relaxation

How do tissues dissipate mechanical stress? My doctoral work established that flow through the spaces between cells can dominate the poroelastic relaxation of multicellular tissues. This connects a tissue’s mechanical response to its intercellular space and fluid transport.

Nature Physics, 2025 ↗MIT News ↗
Engineering platforms

Measuring and perturbing
living systems

I develop micromechanics platforms and combine experiments with finite-element modeling to quantify tissue behavior. My earlier work also explored mechanically driven electroporation, electro-assisted cell printing, and microplasma generation.

Future directions

Physical biomarkers
for aging and disease

My research vision is to pair mechanics with transport measurements in spheroids, organoids, and tissues. I aim to understand how changes in cell organization and fluid pathways contribute to dysfunction, with applications in cancer and brain aging.
